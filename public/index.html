Understanding Channel Congestion

Wi-Fi access points near each other must operate on different frequencies to prevent packet collisions. Stacking APs on the same channel (Co-Channel Interference) behaves like multiple people trying to speak in the same room simultaneously, causing massive slowdowns and disconnections.

Step 1: Emergency

Resolve 5GHz Co-Channel Overlap (Catastrophic)

If a high percentage of your 5GHz access points are locked onto two adjacent channels (for example 40 and 44), APs end up contending for airtime and clients can experience unstable throughput.

Recommended Actions in UniFi Settings (Network 10.0.160):

  • Open your UniFi Controller Network Dashboard (for example https://controller-host:8443).
  • Navigate to your controller's Channel AI / RF Optimization section.
  • Under the 5 GHz Channel Plan section, enable available DFS channels (52 to 136) where allowed in your region.
  • Trigger optimization or manually rebalance AP channels across the expanded channel set.
  • Alternative (Manual Grid Assignment): If you assign channels manually, distribute your 5GHz channels in a grid pattern. Utilize the full spectrum of non-overlapping channels:
    36 40 44 48 52 (DFS) 56 (DFS) 60 (DFS) 64 (DFS) 100 (DFS) 104 (DFS) 108 (DFS) 112 (DFS) 116 (DFS) 120 (DFS) 124 (DFS) 128 (DFS) 132 (DFS) 136 (DFS)
  • Spread adjacent APs physically as far as possible to avoid same-frequency bubbles.
Step 2: High Priority

Balance 2.4GHz Channel Assignments & Power Levels

Our scans show over 22 access points crammed onto **Channel 6**, generating channel utilization rates up to **87%** with significant packet retries. 2.4GHz is highly susceptible to interference.

Recommended Actions in UniFi Settings:

  • Ensure 2.4GHz radios are strictly restricted to the three non-overlapping channels: **1, 6, and 11**. Avoid intermediate channels (like 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10) which cause sideband bleeding.
  • Set the **Transmit Power (TX Power)** of all 2.4GHz radios to **Low** or **Medium** (never High). This reduces the range of the 2.4GHz signal, forcing iPads to steer to the cleaner, faster 5GHz band instead of sticking to slow 2.4GHz from two floors away.
Step 3: Best Practice

Adjust SSID settings to optimize iPad connection

iPads are mobile devices that aggressively look for the strongest signal, but they often struggle to disconnect from a weak distant AP (forming "Sticky Clients").

Recommended Settings for Pupils WiFi Network:

  • Enable **Band Steering** in the Wireless SSID profile. Set it to **Prefer 5G** to push iPads off 2.4GHz.
  • Enable **Fast Roaming (802.11r)** to make transitioning between classroom access points instantaneous for pupils walking between rooms.
  • Set **Minimum RSSI** on classroom APs to `-75 dBm` or `-80 dBm`. This forces a client to disconnect when its signal drops too low, making the iPad instantly connect to the nearest classroom's AP instead of staying sluggishly connected to the AP down the hall.
